## MATH LESSON (15 min)

### Topic: Quadratic Formula and Vertex Form

#### The Quadratic Formula
For `ax² + bx + c = 0`:
**x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a**

The **discriminant** = `b² − 4ac` tells you:
- If positive → 2 real solutions
- If zero → 1 real solution
- If negative → no real solutions

#### Worked example 1
Solve `x² + 4x + 1 = 0`
- a=1, b=4, c=1
- discriminant = 16 − 4 = 12
- x = (−4 ± √12) / 2 = −2 ± √3

#### Vertex Form
**y = a(x − h)² + k** has vertex at **(h, k)**.
- If a > 0: opens up, vertex is minimum
- If a < 0: opens down, vertex is maximum

#### Worked example 2
`y = 2(x − 3)² + 5` has vertex (3, 5), opens up, minimum value = 5.

#### Speed tip
SAT loves: "What is the minimum value?" → It's `k` in vertex form. No calculation needed.

## ENGLISH LESSON (15 min)

### Topic: Semicolons and Colons

#### Semicolon `;` — ONE rule on SAT
**Use a semicolon to join two independent clauses (full sentences).**
`I love SAT prep; it makes me smarter.` ✓ (both sides could stand alone)
`I love SAT prep; even though it's hard.` ✗ ("even though..." is not a full sentence)

**Semicolon = period in disguise.** If you can replace it with a period, it's correct.

#### Colon `:` — Two rules
**Rule A:** After an independent clause, to introduce a list, explanation, or quote.
`She had one goal: to win.` ✓
`I packed three items: bread, cheese, and water.` ✓

**Rule B:** What comes BEFORE the colon must be a full sentence.
`Three items I packed: bread, cheese, and water.` ✓
`I packed: bread, cheese, and water.` ✗ ("I packed" is incomplete — what did you pack?)

#### Dash `—` — Same as comma or colon
A dash can introduce extra info (like comma) or a list/explanation (like colon).
`She had one goal — to win.` ✓

#### Speed tip on SAT
If both sides of the punctuation mark are full sentences → semicolon (or period, or comma+FANBOYS) is right. Colon also works if the second clause explains the first.

**Q5 (SEC).** "The reasons were obvious ____ low pay, long hours, and rude customers."

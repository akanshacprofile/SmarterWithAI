## MATH LESSON (15 min)

### Topic: Systems of Nonlinear Equations

A system with a linear and a quadratic equation can be solved by **substitution**.

#### General method
1. Solve the linear equation for one variable.
2. Substitute into the quadratic.
3. Solve the resulting quadratic.
4. Find the corresponding values of the other variable.

#### Worked example 1
Solve:
```
y = x + 1
y = x² − 1
```
- Set equal: x² − 1 = x + 1 → x² − x − 2 = 0 → (x − 2)(x + 1) = 0
- x = 2 → y = 3; x = −1 → y = 0
- **Solutions: (2, 3) and (−1, 0)**

#### Worked example 2
Solve:
```
x² + y² = 25
y = x + 1
```
- Substitute: x² + (x + 1)² = 25
- x² + x² + 2x + 1 = 25 → 2x² + 2x − 24 = 0 → x² + x − 12 = 0 → (x + 4)(x − 3) = 0
- x = −4 → y = −3; x = 3 → y = 4
- **Solutions: (−4, −3) and (3, 4)**

#### Discriminant interpretation
After substituting, the number of solutions = number of intersections of the line and curve.
- Discriminant > 0: two intersections
- = 0: tangent (one intersection)
- < 0: no real intersection

#### Speed tip
For "how many solutions?" questions, just compute the discriminant after substitution — don't actually solve.

## ENGLISH LESSON (15 min)

### Topic: Run-ons, Comma Splices, and Fragments

#### Run-on sentence
Two independent clauses smashed together with NO punctuation.
- ❌ "I went to the store I bought milk."
**Fixes:** period, semicolon, or comma + FANBOYS.
- ✓ "I went to the store. I bought milk."
- ✓ "I went to the store; I bought milk."
- ✓ "I went to the store, and I bought milk."

#### Comma splice
Two independent clauses joined with ONLY a comma.
- ❌ "I went to the store, I bought milk."
**Fixes:** same as run-on. **Comma alone is never enough.**

#### Fragment
Incomplete sentence (no subject, no verb, or only a dependent clause).
- ❌ "Because she was tired."
- ✓ "Because she was tired, she went to bed."
- ❌ "Running through the field."
- ✓ "She was running through the field."

#### SAT pattern
On punctuation questions, first check: is each side of the punctuation a **complete sentence** (independent clause)?
- Both sides complete → period, semicolon, or `,` + FANBOYS
- Only one complete → comma alone is often OK
- Neither complete → fragment, rephrase needed

#### The "IC test"
Cover one side of the punctuation. Read the other side aloud. Can it stand alone as a sentence? If yes, it's an independent clause.

#### Speed tip
On SEC questions with multiple punctuation options: identify whether each side is IC or not. That tells you which punctuation is legal.

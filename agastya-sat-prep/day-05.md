## MATH REVIEW (15 min)

### Quick recap of Days 1–4

| Skill | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| Factoring quadratics | Find two numbers that multiply to a·c, add to b |
| Quadratic formula | x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / 2a |
| Vertex form | y = a(x−h)² + k → vertex (h, k); k = max/min |
| Ratios | Cross-multiply: a/b = c/d → ad = bc |
| Percent change | New = Original × (1 + r) |
| Exponential growth/decay | y = a · b^x; b = 1 + r (growth) or 1 − r (decay) |
| Compound interest | A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) |

### Common mistakes seen so far
1. **Sign errors** on quadratics — write out factor pairs carefully.
2. **Mixing growth vs decay rate** — "loses 12%" means b = 0.88, NOT 0.12.
3. **Wrong subject** in proportion — units must match across the equals sign.

### Today's drill: spot the error
Each problem below has a worked solution. Find the mistake.

**Drill A:** Solve `x² − 5x + 6 = 0`.
"Need two numbers: multiply to 6, add to 5. So 2 and 3. Factor: (x+2)(x+3). Solutions: x = −2, x = −3."
- **Mistake:** Sum should be −5, so need −2 and −3. Correct factor: (x−2)(x−3). Solutions: x = 2, x = 3.

**Drill B:** "A shirt was $80, now $60. What % off?"
"60/80 = 0.75 = 75% off."
- **Mistake:** 0.75 is what's LEFT, not what's OFF. Discount = 100% − 75% = **25% off**.

## ENGLISH REVIEW (15 min)

### Quick recap of Days 1–4

| Skill | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| Commas | 5 rules: FANBOYS+IC, intro phrase, non-essential info, list of 3+, NOT between subject and verb |
| Semicolons | Joins two full sentences (period in disguise) |
| Colons | After a full sentence, introduces list/explanation |
| Subject-verb agreement | Cross out prepositional phrases; collective nouns = singular; "neither/nor" = use closer |
| Pronoun-antecedent | Each/every/anyone = singular; me vs. I (drop the other person); its (no apostrophe) = possessive |
| Vocab in context | Use the surrounding clue words (contrast, cause, support) |
| Text purpose | Ask: WHY did the author write this sentence? |
| Transitions | Contrast (however), cause (therefore), example (for instance), addition (moreover) |

### Common mistakes seen so far
1. **Comma splice** — two full sentences joined with only a comma is wrong.
2. **Singular indefinite pronouns** — "each, every, neither" trick students into picking "their."
3. **Transition direction** — re-read the sentence with each transition; which one fits the logic?

### Today's drill: spot the error
**Drill A:** "Everyone must turn in their homework."
- **Mistake:** "Everyone" is singular → "his or her" (or rephrase: "All students must turn in their homework.")

**Drill B:** "The team are playing well."
- **Mistake:** Collective noun in American English = singular → "The team **is** playing well."

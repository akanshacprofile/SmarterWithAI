## MATH LESSON (15 min)

### Topic: Ratios, Rates, Proportions

#### Ratios
A ratio compares two amounts. `3:5` means "3 parts to 5 parts."
- 3:5 ratio with 24 of the first → 24/3 = 8 parts each. So 5 parts = 40.

#### Proportions
Two equal ratios. Solve by **cross-multiplying**.
`a/b = c/d` → `ad = bc`

#### Worked example 1
"If 4 pens cost $6, how much do 10 pens cost?"
- 4/6 = 10/x → 4x = 60 → **x = $15**

#### Rates (unit rate)
Always reduce to "per 1" of something.
- "180 mi in 3 hr" → 60 mph (divide both by 3)
- Then multiply by what's asked.

#### Worked example 2
"A printer prints 12 pages in 4 minutes. How long to print 30 pages?"
- Rate: 3 pages/min
- Time: 30/3 = **10 minutes**

#### Speed tip
Set up the proportion so the **units match across the equals sign**: pages/min = pages/min. Mismatched units = wrong answer.

## ENGLISH LESSON (15 min)

### Topic: Subject-Verb Agreement

**Rule:** The verb must match the subject in number.
- Singular subject → singular verb (he runs)
- Plural subject → plural verb (they run)

#### SAT trap 1 — Prepositional phrases
The SAT hides the real subject behind extra words.
- ❌ "The box of chocolates **are** delicious."
- ✓ "The box of chocolates **is** delicious."
The subject is *box* (singular), not *chocolates*.

**Trick:** Cross out the prepositional phrase. Find the noun before "of/with/in/on" → that's the real subject.

#### SAT trap 2 — Compound subjects with "and" vs "or"
- "Tom and Jane **are** here." (and = plural)
- "Tom or Jane **is** here." (or = use the closer noun)

#### SAT trap 3 — Inverted sentences
- "There **is** a book on the table." (subject = book, singular)
- "There **are** books on the table." (subject = books, plural)

#### SAT trap 4 — Collective nouns
*Team, family, group, jury, audience* → usually singular in American English.
- "The team **is** winning."

#### Speed tip
On every SEC question with a verb underlined: (1) find the subject, (2) cross out prepositional phrases, (3) match singular/plural.

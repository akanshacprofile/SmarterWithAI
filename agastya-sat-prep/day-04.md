## MATH LESSON (15 min)

### Topic: Exponential Functions

#### General form
**y = a · b^x**
- `a` = starting value (y when x=0)
- `b` = growth/decay factor
  - b > 1 → growth
  - 0 < b < 1 → decay

#### Growth/decay rate
- "Grows by 5% per year" → b = **1.05**
- "Decays by 8% per year" → b = **0.92** (1 − 0.08)
- "Triples every year" → b = **3**

#### Worked example 1
A population is 200, growing 10% per year. After 3 years?
- y = 200(1.10)³ = 200 × 1.331 = **266.2**

#### Worked example 2
A car worth $20,000 loses 15% of its value per year. After 2 years?
- y = 20000(0.85)² = 20000 × 0.7225 = **$14,450**

#### Compound interest
**A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)**
- P = principal, r = annual rate, n = compoundings per year, t = years
- "Compounded annually" → n = 1, formula reduces to `P(1+r)^t`

#### Speed tip
Read the question — if it says "compounded annually" and gives a percent rate, you can write the formula in 5 seconds. Then plug in.

## ENGLISH LESSON (15 min)

### Topic: Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement & Pronoun Case

#### Rule 1 — Agreement in number
The pronoun must match its antecedent (the noun it replaces) in number.
- "Each **student** must bring **their** book." ❌ (each = singular)
- "Each **student** must bring **his or her** book." ✓ (or rephrase: "All students must bring **their** books.")

#### Rule 2 — Indefinite pronouns are usually SINGULAR
*Each, every, anyone, someone, nobody, either, neither* → singular.
- "Everyone took **his or her** seat."

#### Rule 3 — Pronoun case
- **Subject pronouns** (I, he, she, we, they) → before a verb
- **Object pronouns** (me, him, her, us, them) → after a verb or preposition
- ❌ "Between you and **I**" → ✓ "Between you and **me**" (after preposition)
- ❌ "Me and Tom went" → ✓ "Tom and **I** went"

**Trick:** Drop the other person. "Me went"? No. "I went"? Yes.

#### Rule 4 — Who vs. Whom
- **Who** = subject (replace with "he/she")
- **Whom** = object (replace with "him/her")
- "**Whom** did you call?" → "You called **him**." ✓
- "**Who** called you?" → "**He** called you." ✓

#### Rule 5 — Its vs. It's
- **Its** = possessive ("the cat lost its toy")
- **It's** = "it is" or "it has" ("it's raining")

#### Speed tip
On every pronoun question: (1) find the antecedent, (2) check number, (3) check case (subject vs object).
